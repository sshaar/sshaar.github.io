\cvsection{About Me}

\begin{cvparagraph}
A final-year PhD candidate in Computer Science at Cornell University, advised by Prof.\ Claire Cardie. My thesis, \textit{Long-Form Input Understanding across Vision-and-Language Tasks}, asks what a model needs from a long input: \textbf{what it has to locate, and when locating the right piece is not enough}. I have studied this question in text (TACL 2025) and in short video, for which I built the \textit{MovieRecapsQA} benchmark (CVPR 2026). I am now investigating \textbf{long narrative videos}.
In parallel, I collaborate with NewYork-Presbyterian Hospital on heart-failure projects, ranging from analyzing UNOS exception requests (JHLT 2026) to predictive medication models for heart-failure patients.
Previously, at the Qatar Computing Research Institute, I worked with Prof.\ Preslav Nakov and Prof.\ Giovanni Da San Martino on \textbf{news fact-checking and propaganda detection}.
\end{cvparagraph}

\vspace{-3mm}
\begin{cventries}
  \cvtitleentry
    {Research Interests}
    {
      \begin{cvitems}
        \item \textbf{Long-form input understanding.} What models need from long inputs across text documents, short video, and full-length film: the kind of localization a task requires, and the kind of knowledge it draws on.
        \item \textbf{Benchmarks and evaluation for open-ended generation.} Building the datasets that make model failures observable, and evaluating answers no fixed reference can capture.
        \item \textbf{Clinical NLP.} Using LLMs to surface clinical decision patterns from unstructured medical narratives, with a focus on heart failure and heart transplant care.
        \item \textbf{News fact-checking and propaganda detection} \textit{(prior).} Detecting previously fact-checked claims, and identifying propaganda and persuasion techniques across text, images, and memes.
      \end{cvitems}
    }
\end{cventries}
